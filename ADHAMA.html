About ADHAMA

Two kinds of loneliness, side by side

In the villages and towns where we work, a child has no parent to tuck her in at night, and an elder has no grandchild to sit beside him in the evening. For generations these two people would have belonged to each other. The extended family was the safety net that held the very young and the very old together.

Poverty, disease and migration to the cities have torn that net. Most organizations respond by caring for one group or the other. ADHAMA was founded on a different conviction: the most powerful thing we can do is help them find each other again.

ADHAMA began as a small, community-rooted response to visible and urgent need: children left without caregivers, and elderly people left without support in their final years. Our beneficiaries are not recipients of charity. They are members of a family that circumstance has scattered, and that we are committed to gathering again.

Our mission

ADHAMA mobilizes compassionate action, responsible leadership and sustainable programs to protect vulnerable children, support the elderly, and restore the intergenerational bonds that give both dignity and belonging.

The need

One fracture, two casualties

Across Kenya and the wider region, the extended family once absorbed both orphaned children and ageing relatives. Grandparents raised grandchildren; grandchildren cared for grandparents. That system is under severe strain.

The children Watoto

  • Large numbers of children across the region live without parental care, facing heightened risk of exploitation, malnutrition and interrupted education.
  • Many are cared for by elderly relatives who themselves lack income, health support or strength, which leaves the elder and the child vulnerable together.

The elders Wazee

  • A growing elderly population faces isolation, limited healthcare access, and the absence of younger family members who have migrated to cities.
  • Loss of role and purpose in old age compounds poverty with loneliness and declining wellbeing.

Why separate solutions fall short

When services arrive in separate silos, a grandmother raising three orphaned grandchildren may be visited by one agency for the children and ignored entirely as a caregiver, or supported as an elder while the children in her home go unseen. ADHAMA was built to see the household, and the relationship, as a whole.

Our model

The Intergenerational Restoration Model

Many organizations care for orphans. Many care for the elderly. Almost no one cares for the bridge between them, and the bridge is exactly where the social fracture lies. We treat both crises as a single intervention.

Lasting change happens when immediate care is paired with the restoration of relationships. Three tracks converge on one outcome: families that stay together.

The compound effect

Each track strengthens the other. A child who has a grandparent figure is more emotionally secure and more likely to stay in school. An elder with a child in their life is more active, less isolated, and more engaged with their own care. By investing in the relationship, ADHAMA multiplies the return on every resource invested in either generation.

Programs

Care for both generations, built around the bond between them

Flagship program

Wazee na Watoto Bridge Program “Elders and Children” in Kiswahili

Our signature program pairs children in ADHAMA's care with elders in the community in structured, safeguarded relationships. Children receive mentorship and cultural roots. Elders receive companionship, practical help and a renewed sense of purpose.

Every pairing is screened, supervised and governed by our Safeguarding Policy. Interactions take place in supervised or group settings, with the informed consent of caregivers and guardians.

What a Bridge pair shares

  • Visits Weekly
  • Shared meals Together
  • Storytelling and oral history Heritage
  • Homework support School
  • Joint community activities Belonging

  • Child Welfare and Education

    Safe shelter, nutrition, healthcare referrals, school enrollment and supplies, and psychosocial support, with the goal of connecting every child to at least one lasting intergenerational relationship.

  • Elderly Care

    Home-based and center-based support including health monitoring, nutrition assistance and companionship, with elders invited, as they are able, to serve as mentors, storytellers and guides in the Bridge Program.

  • Kinship-Care Household Support

    Food packages, school fees, health access and caregiver counseling for grandparents and elderly relatives raising orphaned children, so families can stay together rather than be separated into institutions.

  • Food Security and Intergenerational Gardens

    Community gardens tended jointly by elders and children, where traditional farming knowledge is passed down while producing nutritious food for participating households.

  • Counseling and Recovery Support

    Trained psychosocial support for children recovering from loss or abuse and for elders facing grief, illness or isolation, including facilitated group sessions that bring both generations together where appropriate.

Impact

Measuring compound impact

Because we serve both generations together, we can track how improvement in one generation reinforces improvement in the other. Our Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework measures outcomes at three levels.

  • Level 1

    Child

    Safety, stability and development

    Example indicatorsSchool enrollment and retention; wellbeing and trauma-recovery scores

  • Level 2

    Elder

    Dignity, health and connection

    Example indicatorsReported isolation; health access; sense of purpose

  • Level 3

    Relationship & household

    Strength of the intergenerational bond

    Example indicatorsActive pairings sustained; kinship households kept intact; joint activity participation

These levels describe our reporting framework. Current-year results are published in our Annual Report and audited financial statements, provided to all confirmed partners and available on request.

Governance & accountability

Governed with rigor equal to our compassion

An organization entrusted with the care of children and the elderly bears a heightened duty of diligence. ADHAMA operates under a formal Constitution and Board Charter that sets clear roles, term limits, committee structures and codes of conduct.

How we are governed

Each layer carries a distinct function. The Founder's Covenant protects ADHAMA's identity and founding purpose, and grants no authority over day-to-day operations or finances.

Authority flows down
  1. Founder / Founder EmeritusGuardian of the founding vision, mission and values
  2. Board of DirectorsFiduciary and strategic oversight; approves policy and holds leadership to account
  3. Executive LeadershipTurns Board strategy into operational plans and reports to the Board
  4. OperationsDelivers programs and services directly to communities
Accountability flows up

Authority flows down; accountability flows up.

Safeguarding built for intergenerational work

We hold a zero-tolerance policy toward abuse, exploitation and neglect. Because our model deliberately brings adults and children together, safeguarding is designed into every pairing from the start.

  • Elders, staff and volunteers are vetted and screened before taking part.
  • Interactions take place in supervised or group settings.
  • Caregivers and guardians give informed consent.
  • Every child and elder has a clear, confidential way to raise concerns.
  • The Board's Safeguarding Committee reviews the Bridge Program at every meeting.
  • A Whistleblower Policy enables confidential reporting without fear of retaliation.

Financial stewardship

Every shilling, dollar or euro entrusted to us is managed under segregated duties, dual-signatory banking, Board-approved authorization thresholds, and annual independent audits by a qualified, rotating audit firm. Non-political declaration

ADHAMA is strictly non-partisan. We do not endorse or campaign for any political party, candidate or partisan cause, and we never allow our name, resources or platforms to be used for political purposes. Assistance is never conditioned on political support.

Compliance

ADHAMA operates in full compliance with Kenyan NGO regulations and international humanitarian standards, including registration and reporting obligations, applicable labour, financial and child-protection law, and the principles of impartiality, neutrality and accountability to affected populations.

Looking ahead

Our strategic priorities

Guided by our Board-approved Strategic Plan. Partners are welcome to align their support with the priority most meaningful to them.

  1. Establish the Wazee na Watoto Bridge Program as our flagship and grow the number of active, safeguarded intergenerational pairings.

  2. Expand Kinship-Care Household Support so fewer children are separated from grandparents and relatives.

  3. Build a robust compound-impact evidence base through our MEAL framework, and share our learning with the wider sector.

  4. Launch intergenerational community gardens as a model for food security and knowledge transfer.

  5. Develop a replicable Intergenerational Restoration toolkit so the model can be adopted in new communities and by partner organizations.

  6. Diversify our funding base across individual, institutional and corporate partners, and strengthen digital systems for reporting.
